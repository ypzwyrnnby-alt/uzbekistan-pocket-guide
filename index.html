Three things to remember

Samarkand is ancient.
Official tourism material puts its history at more than 2,750 years.
Bukhara is an oasis city.
Its history exceeds 2,500 years and it became a major Islamic and intellectual centre.
Tashkent is a Silk Road city and a Soviet city.
It combines an ancient core with a huge modern metropolis and a distinctive Soviet-era urban legacy.

Uzbekistan in 90 seconds

Antiquity
Sogdian cities such as ancient Samarkand (Afrasiab) flourished in the region.
Silk Road
Trade routes connected Central Asia with China, Iran, India and the Mediterranean. Samarkand, Bukhara and Tashkent became important urban centres.
8th–10th centuries
Islam spread through the region; Bukhara and other cities became centres of scholarship and culture.
13th century
Mongol conquest under Genghis Khan transformed the political landscape.
14th–15th centuries
Amir Timur made Samarkand his capital. His grandson Ulugh Beg became famous for astronomy and scholarship.
16th–19th centuries
The khanates and emirate of Central Asia included the Bukhara, Khiva and Kokand states.
1860s onward
Russian imperial expansion brought Tashkent and then Samarkand into Russian rule.
1924–1991
Uzbekistan existed as a Soviet republic. Soviet planning dramatically reshaped Tashkent and other cities.
1991 →
Uzbekistan became independent on 1 September 1991.

Easy conversation starters

Ulugh Beg
Ask someone about Ulugh Beg's observatory or astronomical tables — he's one of the great intellectual figures associated with Samarkand.
Silk Road
Don't think of it as one road. It was a network of routes linking many cities, peoples and commodities across Eurasia.
